Understanding Ultrasonography: A Non-Invasive Imaging Technique

Ultrasonography, commonly known as ultrasound, is a widely used medical imaging technique that utilizes high-frequency sound waves to produce real-time images of the body’s internal structures. This non-invasive method has become an essential tool in modern medicine, aiding in the diagnosis and monitoring of various medical conditions.

How Ultrasonography Works

Ultrasonography employs sound waves that are transmitted into the body using a handheld device called a transducer. These waves bounce off internal organs, tissues, and fluids, and the returning echoes are converted into images by a computer. Unlike X-rays or CT scans, ultrasound does not use ionizing radiation, making it a safer option for imaging, especially during pregnancy.
